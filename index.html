Pharmaceutical Scientist & Researcher

Md. Nuruzzaman

Md. Nuruzzaman is a pharmacist and researcher with a background that spans pharmaceutical formulation science and behavioural neuroscience. He completed both his Bachelor of Pharmaceutical Sciences and his Master's in Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacy at North South University, Dhaka, where his research on cholinergic memory pathways in rodent models contributed to a peer-reviewed publication in PLOS ONE. Over the past four years, he has built hands-on experience in pharmaceutical research and development at Aristopharma Ltd., working on sterile product formulation, stability studies, and regulatory documentation. He is now seeking doctoral opportunities to pursue advanced research at the intersection of neuropharmacology, drug development, and translational neuroscience.

Publications

Journal Article
PLOS ONE  ·  August 2020
Blockade of the M1 Muscarinic Acetylcholine Receptors Impairs Eyeblink Serial Feature-Positive Discrimination Learning in Mice
Rahman, M. A., Tanaka, N., Nuruzzaman, M., DebNath, S., & Kawahara, S. (2020).
PLOS ONE, 15(8), Article e0237451
Key Findings & Scientific Significance
This study investigated the role of M1 muscarinic acetylcholine receptors (mAChRs) in a higher-order form of associative learning — the eyeblink serial feature-positive discrimination task — using the selective M1 antagonist VU0255035 in mice. The findings demonstrate that blocking M1 receptors significantly impaired the acquisition of conditional discrimination between cued and non-cued trials, as well as overall conditioned response formation. Crucially, M1 blockade did not interfere with the expression of pre-acquired discrimination, establishing a clear distinction between the receptor's role in memory acquisition versus recall. These results parallel those with non-selective scopolamine, implicating M1 receptors specifically as key modulators of cholinergic-dependent memory encoding — with direct relevance to Alzheimer's disease pathophysiology, where M1 receptor loss is a hallmark finding.
